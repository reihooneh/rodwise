Lightning protection, worked out in your browser

Where would lightning strike your building?

Engineers answer that by rolling an imaginary sphere, 20 to 60 metres across depending on how well protected a building must be, over everything on the site. Anything it touches can be struck. Rodwise does that exactly, sends thousands of simulated lightning leaders down to see where they really land, then finds the fewest, cheapest lightning rods that leave nothing for the sphere to touch.

How it works

1

Roll the sphere

The building becomes a grid of heights. Rolling a sphere over it is a grey-scale morphological closing with a ball, so Rodwise can say, for every half-metre of roof, whether the sphere can touch it.

2

Send the lightning down

Each simulated leader has a peak current drawn from the standard's statistics and strikes the first thing within its striking distance, 10 I0.65 metres. Sixty thousand of them show how often each part would be hit.

3

Place the rods

A rod can only ever protect more, never less, so Rodwise adds the one that covers the most exposed roof per dollar, drops any the others make unnecessary, shortens what it can, and checks the result on a finer grid.
